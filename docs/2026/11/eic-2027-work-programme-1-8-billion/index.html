EIC, Defence & Equity

EIC 2027 work programme opens over €1.8 billion for deep tech

The Commission has adopted the EIC's 2027 plan: €578 million for the Accelerator, €248 million for Pathfinder and €100 million for a defence equity call.

Published 5 November 2026 · By Emilio Mulet · Source: European Innovation Council

Key facts

Total
Over €1.8 billion
EIC Accelerator
€578 million (grants below €2.5 million, investments €0.5–10 million)
EIC Pathfinder
€248 million (grants up to €4 million)

€290 million (investments €10–30 million)

€400 million

The European Commission has adopted the European Innovation Council's work programme for 2027, which puts more than €1.8 billion on the table for strategic technologies and for companies that want to scale up. The money is spread across six main schemes, from early-stage research to large equity rounds.

The EIC Accelerator remains the largest line at €578 million for start-ups and SMEs, combining grants below €2.5 million with investments of €0.5 million to €10 million. Pathfinder receives €248 million for multi-disciplinary research teams, with grants of up to €4 million, while Transition has €92 million and grants of up to €2.5 million to turn research results into innovation. A further €25 million goes to the Advanced Innovation Challenges, which pay a €300,000 lump sum.

At the scale-up end, STEP Scale Up has €290 million for investments of €10 million to €30 million, and a dedicated STEP Scale Up Defence call has €100 million with up to €30 million in direct equity. The Scaleup Europe Fund receives €400 million for equity investments above €30 million, reaching up to several hundred million euro.

According to the EIC, the 2027 programme completes the funding for three instruments introduced during 2026: the Scaleup Europe Fund (€1 billion), defence investments (€200 million) and the Advanced Innovation Challenges pilot (€25 million). A redesigned Women TechEU programme doubles its grants to €150,000 per start-up. The EIC will present its 2027 funding opportunities at an online information day on 19 November, followed by a session on the EIC Accelerator Challenges on 20 November.

Why it matters

With the 2027 budget now fixed, applicants can plan their calendar against a known envelope rather than estimates. The Accelerator stays the main entry point for start-ups and SMEs, but the growing weight of equity instruments shows the EIC increasingly expects companies to arrive with an investment case, not only a technology case. Dual-use and defence companies now have a recurring, dedicated route. Teams should read the full work programme and call texts before choosing a scheme, as rules and evaluation steps can change from one year to the next.
